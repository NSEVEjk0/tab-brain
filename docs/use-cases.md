# Ways to use Tab Brain

Tab Brain keeps the readable text, source link, and your own context together. These are practical ways to put that library to work.

## Keep a research project together

Create a collection for the question you are investigating. Save relevant articles, add a short note about the point each one supports, and tag the topics you expect to revisit.

When you return to your writing, search for a phrase from the material. The saved text and original link let you reconnect a useful detail with its source. A page can belong to more than one collection, so the same reading can help several projects.

## Keep the explanation that helped something click

Save a tutorial, API guide, or troubleshooting page after it helps you solve a problem. Add the setup detail that mattered to you: a command, a version, or the step you want to remember.

Later, search the saved text or your note to find that explanation again. Keeping the original source beside it makes the note easier to use when you return to the task.

## Compare the sources behind a choice

Save the product pages, service descriptions, or technical guides you are considering. Select them together and ask a specific question about their captured information.

Tab Brain shows the source snippets before the request goes to Gemini. You review the copy, remove any source you do not want included, and approve the question. The answer brings the selected material together with numbered source links and a list of information it could not find.

You can download that answer and its sources as a research brief to keep with your project.

## Pick up a work session where you left it

Capture useful pages in the Chrome side panel before you finish a session. Add notes about what you learned and what you want to check next, then close the saved tabs.

When you return, your collection, notes, and saved text give you a way back into the work. Pinned tabs and tabs that changed after capture stay open when you use the extension's close action.

## Turn saved reading into a reading plan

Open a saved article and choose when you want to come back to it. Return visits brings together due reminders, upcoming reminders, and unread pages.

You can move a reminder to tomorrow, add it to your calendar, or mark the article as read. This gives saved reading a place in your day instead of leaving it as another open tab.

## Take your reading away from the internet

Capture articles and guides before a journey or a day away from a dependable connection. Their readable text and your notes are stored in the local library.

Read, search, add notes, and organize that material while offline. You can also download a compact reading brief or export a backup to take your saved work with you.

## Keep personal context under your control

Add the reason you saved a page in your own notes. Those notes help local search find the page and stay with it in your library and backups.

When you ask a cloud question, the preview contains the selected source summaries and excerpts. Your personal notes stay outside that source copy. You choose which sources to include and approve the request before it is sent.

## Share a useful result

Use a cloud comparison to make a research brief with source links, or choose bookmarks for a compact reading brief. Preview the reading brief and decide whether to include short summaries and reminder dates.

Download the result and share the file through the tools you already use. Your full library and personal notes stay in the app while you choose the material for that brief.
